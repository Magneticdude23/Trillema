What's actually running under Trilemma

A small-open-economy New Keynesian core — IS/Phillips/Okun, extended with UIP capital flows, a PPP-anchored exchange rate, and an explicit government budget constraint. Each section derives the textbook relationship, then gives the exact stochastic difference equation the sim runs every tick.

01

Output gap — the IS curve

A representative household smooths consumption over time; in an open economy, competitiveness also shifts demand directly.

Derivation

utility$$ E_t \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \beta^k \left[ \frac{C_{t+k}^{1-\sigma}}{1-\sigma} - \frac{N_{t+k}^{1+\varphi}}{1+\varphi} \right] $$

The first-order condition gives the consumption Euler equation:

FOC$$ C_t^{-\sigma} = \beta E_t \left[ C_{t+1}^{-\sigma} \frac{1+i_t}{1+\pi_{t+1}} \right] $$

Log-linearized, and extended with a real-competitiveness term \(q_t\) (Galí–Monacelli small-open-economy NK), this is the open-economy IS curve:

IS$$ y_t = E_t[y_{t+1}] - \frac{1}{\sigma}\big(i_t - E_t[\pi_{t+1}] - r^*\big) + \gamma\, q_t $$

What Trilemma runs

code$$ dy_t = \lambda_y\Big[-\alpha(i_t-\pi_t-r^*) + \gamma\, q_t + g_t\cdot m_{fiscal} + 0.008\,QE_t - d_{cap} - d_{tariff} - y_t\Big]dt + \sigma_y\, dW_t $$
  • \(y_t\)output gap, % deviation from potential
  • \(q_t\)\(\text{clamp}(FX/FX_{fair}-1,\,-0.5,\,1.0)\) — real competitiveness
  • \(g_t \cdot m_{fiscal}\)fiscal stance × the multiplier from §7
  • \(d_{cap}\)\((1-\text{openness})\times 1.2\) — closed-capital-account drag
  • coefficients\(\alpha=0.28,\ \gamma=2.2,\ \lambda_y=0.06\)
02

Inflation — the Phillips curve

Firms face nominal rigidities: only a fraction can reset prices each period (Calvo pricing), so inflation depends on expected future inflation and current marginal cost, proxied by the gap.

Derivation

NKPC$$ \pi_t = \beta E_t[\pi_{t+1}] + \kappa\, y_t $$

Open-economy pass-through adds a depreciation term \(\psi\,\Delta e_t\) — imported goods get more expensive as the currency weakens.

What Trilemma runs

code$$ d\pi_t = \lambda_\pi\Big[\big(\pi^{*} + \phi\, y_t + \psi\,\Delta e_t + 0.03\,\tau_{tariff} + 0.02\,QE_t\big) - \pi_t\Big]dt + \sigma_\pi\, dZ_t $$
  • \(\Delta e_t\)this-step FX % change, clamped ±3%
  • coefficients\(\phi=0.30,\ \psi=9,\ \lambda_\pi=0.045\)
03

Unemployment — Okun's law

empirical

Not derived from optimization — a robust empirical regularity linking cyclical unemployment to the output gap.

code$$ du_t = \lambda_u\Big[\big(u^{*} - \beta_{okun}\, y_t\big) - u_t\Big]dt $$
  • \(u^{*}\)natural rate — your calibration choice
  • coefficients\(\beta_{okun}=0.5,\ \lambda_u=0.05\)
04

Capital flows — uncovered interest parity

The mechanical reason the trilemma holds: the no-arbitrage condition that pins your rate the moment the exchange rate is fixed and capital is mobile.

Derivation

no-arbitrage$$ (1+i_t) = (1+i^{*}_t)\, E_t\!\left[\frac{S_{t+1}}{S_t}\right] $$

Log-linearized:

UIP$$ i_t - i^{*}_t = E_t[\Delta s_{t+1}] $$

With imperfect mobility, a risk premium \(\rho_t\), and a capital-openness dial \(\kappa_{open}\in[0,1]\) standing in for capital controls, a UIP deviation drives a flow instead of an instant rate jump:

Peg the rate and keep \(\kappa_{open}=1\), and \(i_t\) stops being yours — deviating from \(i^{*}_t+\rho_t\) just drains reserves through \(KA_t\) until the peg gives way. That's the whole trilemma, in one line.

What Trilemma runs

code$$ KA_t = \text{clamp}\Big(\kappa_{open}\cdot 0.5\,(i_t-i^{*}_t-\rho_t)\ -\ (2\,\text{fear}_t + 4\,\text{attack}_t),\ -10,\ 10\Big) $$

\(\rho_t\) is the composite risk premium from §9; fear/attack are speculative-attack state variables that force outflows regardless of the rate differential.

05

Exchange rate — purchasing power parity

Derivation

The law of one price, aggregated across a goods basket: the exchange rate tracks relative cumulative price levels.

price levels$$ P_t = P_{t-1}\Big(1+\frac{\pi_t}{100}\Big),\qquad P^{*}_t = P^{*}_{t-1}\Big(1+\frac{\pi^{*}_t}{100}\Big) $$
PPP$$ S^{fair}_t = S_0\cdot\frac{P_t}{P^{*}_t} $$

What Trilemma runs

Float — pulled toward PPP fair value, pushed by the BOP flow (§6), nudged by QE:

code$$ dS_t = \Big[(S^{fair}_t - S_t)\cdot 0.05 - \text{clamp}(BOP_t,\pm 8)\cdot 0.5 - 0.01\,QE_t\Big]dt + \sigma_S\,dW_t $$

Peg / managed — reserves absorb the imbalance instead, scaled by peg hardness \(h\) (1 hard, 0.5 managed, 0 float):

code$$ \Delta\text{Reserves}_t = \text{clamp}(BOP_t\cdot h\cdot 0.5,\ \pm 6)\,dt $$
Reserves hitting zero isn't a scripted event — the peg mechanically cannot be defended once this equation runs out of room, and forces a devaluation.
06

Balance of payments

An accounting identity, not a behavioral one — current + capital account must sum to the change in reserves (peg) or to zero by construction (float).

identity$$ BOP_t \equiv CA_t + KA_t = NX_t + KA_t $$

Net exports follow Marshall–Lerner: a real depreciation helps the trade balance when demand elasticities are large enough.

code$$ NX_t = \text{clamp}\Big(\chi\, q_t + 0.03\,\tau_{tariff} - 0.05\,R_t - 0.3\,y_t + \text{ToT}_t,\ \pm 8\Big) $$
  • \(R_t\)accumulated foreign retaliation against your tariffs
07

Fiscal multiplier & automatic stabilizers

Derivation

From \(Y=C+I+G+NX\), with \(C=c(1-\tau)Y\) and imports \(M=mY\), an autonomous shock \(\Delta G\) implies:

$$ \Delta Y = \Delta G + c(1-\tau)\Delta Y - m\,\Delta Y $$

Solving for \(\Delta Y/\Delta G\):

multiplier$$ m_{fiscal} = \frac{1}{1-c(1-\tau)+m} $$

Differentiating \(T=\tau Y\) gives the automatic-stabilizer identity — output falls, tax revenue falls with it, deficit widens with no policy action:

stabilizer$$ \frac{dT}{dY} = \tau \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{autoDeficit}_t = -\tau\, y_t $$

What Trilemma runs

code$$ m = 0.20 + 0.15\cdot\text{openness},\qquad m_{fiscal} = \frac{1}{1-0.6(1-\tau)+m} $$

More open economies get a smaller multiplier — more of every unit of stimulus leaks abroad through imports.

08

Public debt dynamics

Derivation

The government budget constraint: next period's debt equals this period's plus interest, plus the primary deficit.

$$ B_{t+1} = (1+r_t)B_t + G_t - T_t $$

Divided by nominal GDP, continuous-time limit — the standard debt-sustainability identity:

r > g$$ d\big(b_t\big) = \text{pd}_t + b_t\,(r_t - g_t)\,dt $$

Debt shrinks when growth outpaces the borrowing rate, and can spiral even under a primary surplus if a recession crushes \(g\) hard enough.

What Trilemma runs

code$$ \Delta b_t = (g_t + \text{autoDeficit}_t)\Big(\frac{dt}{365}\Big) + b_t\left[\frac{r_t}{100}\cdot\frac{dt}{365} - \Big(y^{real}_t + \frac{\pi_t}{100}\cdot\frac{dt}{360}\Big)\right] $$
Sovereign default triggers above \(b_t=220\%\). \(r_t\) itself is derived in §9.
09

Risk premium & borrowing rate

empirical

A reduced-form composite reflecting the sovereign-risk literature — reserve adequacy, debt load, capital controls, and FX-debt exposure are each independently linked to sovereign spreads.

code$$ \rho_t = 4\max(0,0.5-\tfrac{\text{res}_t}{\text{res}_0}) + 0.10\max(0,\pi_t-6) + 2\,\text{fear}_t + 3\,\text{attack}_t + 0.8(1-\kappa_{open}) + \tfrac{\max(0,FXDebt_t)}{100} + 0.012\max(0,b_t-60) $$

The effective borrowing rate drifts toward the policy rate plus that spread — but compresses under QE (the portfolio-balance channel that is QE's transmission mechanism) and rises slightly with active deficit spending:

code$$ r_t^{target} = i_t + 0.5 + 0.018\max(0,b_t-60) + 0.15\,\rho_t - 0.012\,QE_t + 0.15\max(0,g_t) $$
10

Numerical implementation

A set of coupled SDEs, integrated with Euler–Maruyama at a base step of \(dt=1\) simulated day (batched up to 200 micro-steps per frame at higher speeds). Noise scales drift by \(k=dt/3\) and diffusion by \(\sqrt{k}\), calibrated at a 3-day reference step — so changing the step size doesn't change the process's statistics, as a proper SDE discretization requires.

Reflects the live build. If the calibration changes, this page should change with it.
